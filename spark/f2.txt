In Humankind: A Hopeful History, Rutger Bregman asks what happens when something similar occurs outside fiction. 
In 1965, six Tongan schoolboys became stranded on the uninhabited island of ‘Ata. 
They survived for more than a year by cooperating. 
They collected rainwater, cultivated food, kept a fire burning, and divided the work among themselves. For Bregman, their survival offered a welcome answer to Golding’s pessimism.
